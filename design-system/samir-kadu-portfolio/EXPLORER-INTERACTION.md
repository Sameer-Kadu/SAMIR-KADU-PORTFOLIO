# Explorer View — Interaction Language

Derived from frame-by-frame analysis of the supplied reference clip
(`__title_Monochrome_Space.mp4`, 10.01s, 1280×720, 24fps — 240 frames sampled at
4fps plus six full-resolution key frames).

The reference defines **interaction quality**, not content. Nothing from it is
copied: no assets, no geometry, no environment, no colour treatment.

---

## What the reference actually does

### Scene progression (the spine)

| Time | Beat |
|---|---|
| 0.0–2.0s | Already in warp. Radial streaks from a vanishing point in the windscreen. |
| 2.0–3.0s | **Deceleration.** Streaks collapse into a static starfield. Strong ease-out. |
| 3.0–4.5s | Deep space. Galaxy band fills the view. Camera drifts. |
| 4.5–6.0s | A star enters frame left, blooming. Light becomes the subject. |
| 6.0–8.0s | Solar system reveals — planets march across the window. |
| 8.0–10.0s | Camera has yawed right and pitched down; console fills the lower third. |

The story is **arrival**. Every beat adds information the previous beat did not
have. There is no repetition and no loop.

### Camera language

- **The camera never stops.** Even in the calm section it yaws slowly (~6–9°/s)
  and pitches down a few degrees over the clip. Static framing never happens.
- **No linear timing anywhere.** The warp deceleration is a hard ease-out;
  the pan is near-constant with very long soft ends.
- Movement is a slow *reveal* — content enters frame rather than being cut to.
- No jump cuts. Not one in ten seconds.

### The cockpit

Roughly **30–35% of the screen** is structure; the rest is window.

- Central trapezoidal windscreen, wider at the top, tapering down.
- Two large angled side windows.
- Small upper panels above the main screen.
- Mullions are thick, near-black, with a faint lit inner edge.
- Console across the bottom ~30%: a large circular radar dead centre as the
  focal instrument, flanked by multi-function displays, icon panels and button
  grids that angle away toward the edges.

The frame is what sells *presence* — it establishes that you are inside
something, looking out.

### HUD

Deliberately **almost invisible**. Low-contrast technical labels sit on the
glass, with tick-mark scales flanking centre, small boxed numerics, and thin arc
brackets framing the central view. It reads as instrumentation, never as game
UI. Nothing competes with the view.

### How attention is directed

**With light, not with UI.** The star bloom pulls the eye left; the planets
entering pull it right. No arrow, no label, no "click here" ever appears.
Brightness and motion do the guiding.

### Visual hierarchy

window content → cockpit silhouette → console instruments → HUD text

## What we take (principles, not pixels)

1. **Open in motion.** Explorer View begins mid-flight in warp and decelerates
   into the scene, rather than fading up on a static shot.
2. **The camera always drifts.** A continuous idle yaw/pitch underneath the
   mouse-look, so the scene is never frozen.
3. **A real canopy, not a vignette.** Faceted frame plus a console, occupying
   the lower third — presence comes from structure.
4. **Light guides, text does not.** Earth's hover lift, the star bloom and the
   landing-pad glow are the wayfinding.
5. **Anticipation before acceleration.** The click pulls back slightly before
   the thrust, so the launch has a windup.
6. **Nothing snaps.** Every state change is damped or eased.

## What we deliberately do differently

- **Colour, not monochrome.** The reference is greyscale by design; ours keeps
  the portfolio's cool blue accent so the two views read as one product.
- **The destination is interactive.** The reference is a linear film. Ours hands
  control to the visitor — Earth is a target you find and choose.
- **The journey has a second half.** The reference ends at the planets. Ours
  lands and becomes an explorable world built from the portfolio data.
